# Murao Honorific - Draft

---

The Murai have an honorific system to address others

---

The idea is for the honorifics are suffixes. They are added to the end of a name. Ommission of the honorific is dehumanizing. Three basic ones are - san. - xan, and - ton. - san is used on any adult, like sir. Maam, - xan (chan) is for children. - ton is for any stranger, unknown person. Rude if used on a known person 

---

-ton is purely neutral. There is no relationship established. - san and - xan are neutral to positive. An adult or child who are not misbehaving. For a bad adultnor child. - ban and - min are used. 

---

So far, i have mentioned informal honorifics. These are subjective. What the speaker hold their relation with the subject to be. An annoying child may be referred Aleri-min by the teacher, but Aleri-xan by the nanny. 

---

the first formal honorific we will discuss will be -banim.  this is used for criminals. specifically convicted criminals. it is proper to use -banim for a convicted criminal from the time of their conviction, up to 5 years after serving their sentence. after that, one can use nay honorific that applies, or continue using -banim. also, while one can use it on someone accused of a crime, using -ban is preferred 

---

Next we have -tan. This is used for a friend, like a buddy, bff. Then is -kun, this is an affectionate term for a partner, courtship, girlfriend, boyfriend, or spouse. Then it is the formal -lin. This one is for a wedded spouse. While -kun and -lin may be interchangeable, they are like referring yiur partner as my honey muffin or my wife. -lin can only be used after the wedding ceremony. 

---

Family/kinship:

-tun: family member, informally used for close individuals, like family. "Aresa-tun is over there." Aresa is family to the speaker
-tam: older generation in the family. parents, aunts, uncles, grandparent. from a child, it refers to adults in the family.
-tama: my parents
-tamo: my grandparents, or informally, to affectionally refer to an elderly person

-tin: a child in my family. my own child, niece, nephew
-tini: my own child, my son, my daughter.
-tina: my eldest child
-tinina: my youngest, my baby. informally to babies, infants.
-tino: my grandchild. informally, used by elderly to reference children

-tan: my close friend, like family
-tunu: My sibling, My brother, my sister.
-tuno: my eldest sibling
-tuni: my youngest sibling

---

because of the more specific honorifics, -tun generally defaults to either family of my generation, including my siblings, or to just cousins, and informally to a friend. 
"Aresa-tun is over there." Aresa is family to the speaker. She can be anywhere from an elder to a newborn. but more generally, Aresa is of my generation, my sibling or cousin. if used officially or if i am more specific toward others, then she is my cousin. "Aleri-tunu and Aresa-tun are over there." since i define Aleri more specifically, specially since aleri is of my generation, Aresa is my cousin. 
"Aleri-tamo and Aresa-tun are over there." Aleri is an elder to me. Aresa is of my generation, more likely cousin.
if talking to a guard, an official setting "Aleri-tamo and Aresa-tun are over there." Aresa is my cousin. 

---

-dan is for a person that is my superior. can be a boss, group leader, but can also be a teacher, even parent. in an informal setting, i can refer as someone as superior even if it is not a formal relationship, like an elder, a retired person. "Aleri-dan is over there"

-dara is for an office of governance, specifically the chief executive, like mayor, chief, governors. it also applies to a judge. in informal setting, other government officials would prefer this honorific, such as legislators, tax collectors, law enforcement, even city clerks. "Aleri-dara will give a speech"

-dairan are for heads of state. Kings, Pharaoh, emperors, Presidents. "Aleri-dairan will give a speech"

-dairani is not just a head of state , but my ruler, my sovereign.  who i a subject of. "Aleri-dairani will give a speech"

Military: Sor, Sir, Ser, Sur, Sar, Sori, Sore, Soro, Sora, Sorain
The military is a bit different. unlike the others which depends as the relationship the speaker has with the subject. in with the military, their is acknowledgement on what they are, regardless or irrelevant of any other relationship. like acknowledging rank. Arisa-Sore is a warrior, but possesses no information on the relationship i have with Arisa, it is purely her accomplishment of achieving the rank of warrior. as such the honorifics used depend if i am part of the military or not. Sor and Sar are general honorifics. Sor being any military, friend or foe, and Sar being allied, friendly, my own troops, comrade. in a formal setting, this is the best a civilian can accomplish without referencing rank.
this culture has four ranks: Soldier, Warrior, Knight and Noble. Archnoble is a special supreme general rank that is still of Noble rank. Sori, Sore, Soro and Sora map to these ranks. if a civilian knows the rank, it is best to use the proper honorific. Arisa-sor is a member of a military (seeing a fighter passing by). Arisa-sar is of a friendly military (like a local guard), Arisa-sore is of Warrior rank (i know her rank). if within the military, all this still aplies, with a few more honorifics. the reletional rank honorifics: Sir, Ser, Sur. it depend of my rank compared to the subject's rank. if i am a Warrior, Arisa-sir is a knight or noble, Arisa-ser is also a warrior. Arisa-sur is a soldier. informaly, i can refer as Sar to my comarades, battle buddies, military friends, regardless of rank. Sor anyone else, like rival units. it is important that this is confined in informal settings as failing to recognized a rival that is friendly (informal Sor) as friendly unit (formal Sar) can lead to operational failure. 

Sorain is the honorifc for the ArcNoble, the Supreme General. but is can also be used to refer to the mission lead. the one that is currently issuing my mission orders. not the one that is telling me what to do, but the one who made the decition to move the formation there. as for all intends and pouposes, they are my Sorain. i do not follow orders from other if it is not my Sorain and thier officer. even if a higher ranking general of a support unit tells me to do something else. as long as it is not from the archnoble themselves or my Mission Lead, i am not obligated to obay them. might even get reprimanded if i do. but is still have discretion to disobey my Sorain, but is them whom i am disobeying.  and from the military perspective. San is a friendly civilian, and Ton is an unknown person could be civilian, military, friend or foe. the usual politeness of defaulting to San is disregarded. as one should not designate a person as friendly, if they could potentially be foe

animal honorifics. there are two honorifics, one formal, and one informal. -pet is the formal. it applies to animals owned by the person. they are responsible for that animal. "Fili-pet found a coin!" meaning "Fili, my working animal, found a coin". -pit is informal, it is used for a beloved animal, a companion animal, like a pet. "Fili-pit found a coin!" means "Fili, my pet, found a coin." or "Good boy Fili found a coin." since they are animals, they do not need to have honorifics. "Fili found a coin" is perfectly acceptable. Fili could be the neighbors animal, but without an honorifics, one is not claiming neither ownership, nor responsibility over that animal. "The dog found a coin" is also acceptable. if Fili is a stray, and referred to as Fili-pit, because it is a beloved animal to you, one may be claiming responsibility if Fili the stray digs up the neighbors flower bed.

A bit meta:

one aspect in design was the vowel choice. A few of the honorifics with an "i" are for children, lower. Like rin, rini, ri, sori, some are negative, like Min, Banim. Some 

honorifics with an "a" are for positives, higher. Tan is not a stranger, dan is a superior. Daitan is the ruler, sar is a friendly military, sora is the highest rank, noble. Soran is the highest of the high ranks. Xan is a good child, san is a being polite to that person.

another important consideration in its design is letter count and syllable length. most are 3 letter, 1 syllable in length, that is the norm. the learning honorifics are 2 letters: Ra, Re, Ri. these are the shortest ones. it denotes its humbleness. to be called teacher, student, or peer over anything else is humbling. calling them Aleri-ra over Aleri-dan, a teacher over a superior. four letter, two syllable honorifics: Tini, Tunu, Tama. My Child, sibling and parents are very important relationships in my family. Tin, Tun, and Tam still apply to the person. child of my family, someone in my family and adults in my family. Aleri-tam is an adult, or prior generation , in my family, like my parents. Aleri-tama is my parent. Sori, Sore, Soro and Sora refer to each of the four ranks in the military of this culture. it is important to recognize their rank, their achievements., not just simply that they are military, Aleri-sir is my superior officer, Aleri-soro is a Knight, could be higher equal or lower than mine, but being a knight is Aleri's accomplishment. five letter, two syllable honorifics Banim - over anything else, they are criminals. six letter, two syllable honorifics: Raidan, Dairan, Sorain. above all else, they are the masters of masters, the generals of all troops, the ultimate authority of the land.
