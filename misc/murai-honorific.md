## 1. General / Behavioral

- **-san** – Polite adult (or unknown-age person). Neutral to positive.
    
- **-xan** – Polite child / minor, well-behaved or neutral.
    
- **-dor** – Stranger / unknown person. No relationship yet.

---

## 2. Romance / Like-Family

- **-tan** – Like-family friend, close friend, “buddy / best friend.” No kinship, but emotionally family.
    
- **-kun** – Romantic partner (girlfriend/boyfriend/spouse). Affectionate; appropriate in familial settings, rude in formal ones.
    
- **-lin** – Wedded spouse. Can only be used after the wedding ceremony.
    

---

## 3. Family / Kinship

### Generic family

- **-tem** – Generic “you are family to me,” any generation, any branch.
    

### Older generations (before me)

- **-tam** – Prior generation in my family: parents’ generation and above (aunts, uncles, grandparents, great-grandparents, etc.).
    
- **-tamo** – My parents.
    
- **-tama** – My grandparents.
    

### My generation

- **-tun** – My generation in the family: siblings, cousins, etc.
    
- **-tunu** – My sibling (neutral).
    
- **-tuno** – My eldest sibling (respect/affection; can still be called **-tunu**).
    
- **-tuni** – My youngest sibling (respect/affection; can still be called **-tunu**).
    

### Younger generations (after me)

- **-tin** – Next generations in my family: children, grandchildren, nieces/nephews, cousins’ children, etc.
    
- **-tini** – My child.
    
- **-tina** – My eldest child (respect/affection; can also be **-tini**).
    
- **-tinina** – My youngest child / my baby (respect/affection; can also be **-tini**).
    
- **-tino** – My grandchild.

## 4. Mentorship / Learning / Religious

- **-ra** – Someone I learned from.
    
- **-re** – Someone I learned with (peer learner).
    
- **-ri** – Someone who learned from me.
    
- **-raidan**
    
    - Familial/subjective: someone I aspire to be like, my ideal.
        
    - Formal: a recognized master / expert in their field.

## 5. Authority / Governance / Status

- **-dan** – My superior (boss, leader, teacher, respected elder).
    
- **-dara** – Holder of an office of governance: chief, mayor, governor, judge; informally also other officials.
    
- **-dairan** – Head of state (king, emperor, president, etc.).
    
- **-dairani** – _My_ sovereign, the ruler I am subject to.
    

---

## 6. Military – Structure, Rank, Allegiance

### Allegiance / type

- **-sor** – Any military person (friend or foe, unknown allegiance).
    
- **-sar** – Friendly / allied military, own-side forces.
    
- **-surin** – Enemy military / hostile combatant.
    

### Ranks

- **-sori** – Soldier (lowest rank).
    
- **-sore** – Warrior (second rank).
    
- **-soro** – Knight (third rank).
    
- **-sora** – Noble rank (fourth rank).
    
- **-sorain** – Archnoble / Supreme Commander; also used for the mission lead whose orders define _my_ mission.
    

### Relational (inside the military)

- **-sir** – Military superior (they outrank me).
    
- **-ser** – Military equal (same rank as me).
    
- **-sur** – Military inferior (I outrank them).

## 7. Animals

- **-pet** – Animal I own / am responsible for (working animal, mount, etc.).
    
- **-pit** – Beloved companion animal, “pet” in the emotional sense.

Here’s your D-class social ladder, laid out clean and in order, using only what you’ve already established.

---

## The D-Hierarchy (from lowest to highest)

### **1. –dor — Stranger / Unplaced Person**

* **Meaning:** I don’t know who you are or what you are to me.
* No friendliness, no hostility—just *unknown*.
* Example:

  * “Aleri-dor is over there.” → That person is a stranger.

This is the **zero point** of the social graph.

---

### **2. –dil — Inferior / Subordinate**

* **Meaning:** You are *below me* in status, rank, or role.
* Technically correct, but considered **rude or harsh**.
* Most polite people just use **–san** for subordinates.
* Example:

  * Proper but cold: “Arin-dil, file the report.”
  * Politer alternative: “Arin-san, file the report.”

**Bosses who use –dil** are making a point:
*“Never forget I’m above you.”*

---

### **3. –san — Neutral Person / Default Politeness**

Not a D-honorific, but socially it sits here:

* Used instead of –dil for people beneath you (to avoid rudeness).
* Used for anyone you’re treating as “a proper person,” regardless of rank.

So the “base civil layer” is:

> dor → san (or xan for kids) → then up or down from there

---

### **4. –dan — Superior / Above Me**

* **Meaning:** You stand above me in some hierarchy.
* Boss, leader, teacher, respected elder, mentor in a position of authority.
* Context chooses which hierarchy matters: workplace, school, clan, etc.
* Example:

  * “Aleri-dan is my teacher.”
  * “Arane-dan is my unit leader.”

This is the everyday **respectful upward** honorific.

---

### **5. –dara — Office of Governance**

* **Meaning:** You hold a formal position of civic/government authority.
* Chief, mayor, governor, judge.
* Other officials (clerks, tax collectors, administrators) *like* to be addressed this way even when it’s a stretch.
* Example:

  * “Aleri-dara will hear the case.” (judge)
  * “Enar-dara is the town chief.”

This marks **official power**, not just social respect.

---

### **6. –dairan — Head of State (Any Nation)**

* **Meaning:** You are *a* head of state.
* King, pharaoh, emperor, president, etc.
* In Murai society, the head of state *is* the ArchNoble (the top of the noble/military ladder).
* Example:

  * “Atenea-dairan will speak at dawn.” → Atenea is **a** head of state.

This is a **title-level** honorific: rank at the top of the political pyramid.

---

### **7. –dairani — My Sovereign**

* **Meaning:** You are *my* head of state.
  The one I owe allegiance to (or am supposed to).
* Relational, not absolute: it encodes **loyalty**, not just status.

The distinction is sharp:

* “Atenea-dairan” → Atenea is a head of state (fact).
* “Atenea-dairani” → Atenea is *my* sovereign (allegiance).

You spelled out the social consequence:

> Failing to use **–dairani** for your own ArchNoble can signal that your loyalty lies elsewhere.

* Proper:

  * “I am at your service, Atenea-dairani.”
* Improper (if she is your ArchNoble):

  * “I am at your service, Atenea-dairan.”

That’s like telling your own monarch:
“I acknowledge your title, but not you as *my* ruler.”

---

## In short: the D-ladder

From the speaker’s perspective:

* **-dor** → I don’t know you.
* **-dil** → You are beneath me.
* **-san** → You are a person I’m treating neutrally/politely.
* **-dan** → You are above me.
* **-dara** → You are an official.
* **-dairan** → You are a head of state.
* **-dairani** → You are *my* sovereign.

It’s a vertical map of social distance and power, with **-dairani** as the top not just of rank, but of *claimed allegiance*.
