# Imoh

In the World of Threa, there are 21 races. This is a description of the Imoh, a race that lives in the world of Threa.

## Appearance

The Imoh are relatively short, no more than 5 feet tall. They have snow-white skin. Their hair and eyes are vibrant reds, oranges, and violets. They have bushy eyebrows. Males may grow mustaches. Their hands resemble mittens, with one broad finger and a thumb. They have two-toed feet.

Their clothing selection includes sandals with socks, loafers, boots, shorts, pants, and short-sleeve shirts. Headbands are also common. Elders wear a blue vest. Their pants, shorts, boots, and sandals tend to be brown; everything else can be white, light gray, or light blue, with some red trim. Because of their unique temperature sensitivity, they feel comfortable wearing what looks like summer attire even in freezing temperatures.

### Temperature Sensitivity

An interesting property of the Imoh is that their comfort level regarding temperature is significantly lower than average. Even in freezing cold temperatures, they may just feel a bit chilly. As such, their clothing appears more suited for hot summer rather than the cold environment they live in—sandals, shorts, and short-sleeve shirts. Because their normal room temperature is below freezing, an actual room temperature setting would be hot for them. A possible consequence is that they would feel sweltering hot in what otherwise would be an average room temperature. However, since they have not ventured beyond the Aurora Mountains (called the Blue Mountains from their side), this remains unconfirmed.

## Geography and Isolation

The Imoh live in near-complete isolation from the rest of Threa. They inhabit the Southern Snowfield, located on the other side of an impassable mountain range and encircled by harsh, nearly unnavigable water.

### The Aurora Mountains (Blue Mountains)

On the northwestern side of the Southern Snowfield lies a nearly impassable mountain range, referred to as the **Blue Mountains** from the snowfield side of the range. From the rest of Threa, they are referred to as the **Aurora Mountains**. Ages ago, some intrepid explorers passed through the Aurora Mountains and made first contact with the Imoh. Further exploration led to the discovery of rare ores and gems in the snowfield side of the mountains. However, transporting ore and gems through the mountain pass proved impractical (the crossing is comparable to crossing the Alps but worse).

### The Southern Snowfield

The Southern Snowfield is a vast, snow-covered prairie, mostly flat with some snow dunes and hills here and there, but no mountains. All sides other than the northwestern mountains are surrounded by raging waters. The coast is actually a 10-story sheer cliff drop. The sea floor near the coast plunges into deep trenches. This network of trenches is what makes the water surrounding the snowfield so fierce.

Below the thick layers of snow and ice lies fertile soil and clean water. The roots of crops cut through the snow and ice to reach the soil beneath. However, in some areas, the snow has compacted into ice near the soil level, making it difficult to farm.

### The Subglacial Delta

Where the subglacial delta emerges and empties into the sea, it has carved caverns. The "coast" of the snowfield is marked by a network of dangerous waters, but a passage exists: the **Blue Week**. Once a year, for about one week near the end of autumn, a narrow stream in the sea is calm enough to not break ships. Outsiders have used this corridor to establish a foothold on the Southern Snowfield.

## Society and Governance

### Tribal Structure

The Imoh are a simple patriarchal tribal society. They are organized into tribes, each governed by a single **Elder**, who is always male. His wife can provide counsel if needed.

The Elder chooses their successor and decides when they will retire. If an Elder passes without a successor, the eldest person in the tribe becomes the Elder. However, they may deem themselves too old to govern and name a successor before retiring immediately. Some individuals attempt to curry favor with the Elder hoping to be chosen, but this never works.

Because of their isolation, there is little conflict. There is plenty of space for the tribes to use. They have no military or police force. Inter-tribal interaction is encouraged, and many families are mixed-tribe families.

### Martial Tradition

The Imoh have no combat classes due to their peaceful environment and small tribal populations. However, they do uphold a martial tradition focused on meditation and demonstration matches rather than actual combat. These demonstration matches are individual performances rather than fights, evaluated based on skill, composition, creativity, and precision—similar to dance.

### Festivities and Culture

The Imoh hold various festivities, competitions, and meditative sessions throughout the year, facilitating inter-tribal connection and cultural exchange.

## Buildings and Infrastructure

### Imoh Dwellings

The primary building material for Imoh structures is compacted snow. Their settlements are recognized by dome igloos, but the living space is not in the dome—that serves as the ceiling. Living space is dug approximately one story below the snow level. The extracted snow is used to make compact snow bricks that line the under-snow walls and dome ceilings. The entrance to dwellings is typically a stairway near one of the domes that leads below ground.

## Agriculture and Food Systems

### Farming

The Imoh's farming is unique since they live in a vast field of snow. The process is as follows:

1. **Uncovering:** They first need to uncover the ground to access soil for planting.
    
2. **Field Preparation:** Farm fields are dug about two stories below snow level—note that Imoh buildings are already one story deep, so adjacent farm buildings are one story deep, while the field itself is two stories deep. Fields are dug to open sky (not caverns) to maximize sunlight during the seedling phase.
    
3. **Seeding:** Berry and cottonberry seeds are sown into the farm field.
    
4. **Root Growth:** When seeds germinate, their roots shoot downward, cutting through multiple stories of snow. However, if the snow has compacted into ice that is too hard or solid, roots cannot reach the soil and fail to take root. It takes some days—no more than a month—for roots to reach the soil. Throughout this time, farmers must tend the crops and provide nutrients while roots travel downward.
    
5. **Taking Hold:** Once roots reach soil, the first leaves form, indicating the roots have taken hold.
    
6. **Covering:** The field is then buried under snow in a structured manner so that light passes through translucent layers of snow and ice. At this point, the field requires minimal maintenance.
    
7. **Harvesting:** Once ready to harvest, the field is uncovered again, allowed a day or so of open-sky sunlight, then the berries are harvested.
    

**Frost and Farmability:** A key challenge is knowing how to find **farmable snow**—where it is soft enough for berry roots to cut through. If the snow buildup is too thick and has compacted into ice, it is not farmable.

### Crops and Resources

The Imoh harvest mainly **berries** and **cottonberries**. They also cultivate wild **coconut bushes** (actually small trees with tall, strong trunks) buried under the snow. The Imoh know where these are located, uncover them, and harvest coconuts. The coconut husks provide fibers, as do cottonberries, which are farmed for textile production.

### Fishing

The Imoh are expert fishers. They break the ice over frozen water and fish. They very easily get large catches but only take what they need.

## Wildlife

### Snow Ferrets

The **snow ferret** is related to the common dark-tailed ferret that populates throughout Threa. While the common ferret's winter coat is seasonal, the snow ferret's white winter coat is year-round. The winter coat has varieties: the light fur can be off-white, light gray, or sheen white, while the tips may be dark gray, burnt brown, or pitch black. The sheen white/pitch black contrast coat has significant value in the textile market of Threa.

Snow ferrets are one of the few pests that threaten Imoh farm fields. They dive about 3 feet into the snow around bushes to snack on ice ants (see below).

Some hunters from outside the snowfield come thinking the year-round availability would make it easier to catch valuable pelts. Some attempt to bring common ferrets into the snowfield, hoping the frigid cold would trigger and set their winter coat. However, these ferrets are not accustomed to the extreme cold and do not survive—not even long enough for the winter coat to set in.

### Ice Ants

Ice ants are one of the few insects that exist in the snowfield. They crawl along the trunks of the coconut and berry bushes deep beneath the snowpack. They carve out spaces between the trunk and the snow, moving nutrients and bioluminescent light along the trees.

### Emperor Bears

Emperor bears are massive predators native to the snowfield. Their mating season is in the middle of winter. The black-tipped tail makes finding each other easier in white-out conditions. Pups are born at the beginning of spring, when the storms "calm down," giving them as much time as possible to grow hardy enough to survive their first winter.

## Outsider Contact and Development

### Discovery and the Gold Rush

Explorers eventually discovered a sea passage around the mountains. This led to the establishment of **Blue Hold**, a port town on the Southern Snowfield. However, the mines (called **Coldstone**) are on the opposite side of the snowfield from Blue Hold. The long, arduous **Frost Rush Trail** was established to connect Coldstone and Blue Hold.

### Blue Hold Port Town

Blue Hold is an impressive feat of engineering made of three parts: the Dock, Steps, and Warehouse.

#### Construction of the Dock

To even build the dock, outsiders had to tear down the cliff face sitting on it. Two ships filled with nothing but fuel were set ablaze and crashed into the cliffs. The intense heat of the two fireballs weakened and melted the snowpack. Eventually, it gave way, and the cliff face came crashing down to the sea. This created space at the shore that eventually became the dock.

However, this construction had consequences: Emperor bear pups were in their den when the cliffs crashed down. Three mama bears, in a revenge spree, boarded two ships from the fleet. Those ships carried builders, materials, and ranking members. Both ships were lost with all hands. One mama bear survived, helped by the newly created coastal surface. She could not have made the climb in her weakened state and rested on the new shore, halting all construction plans. No one dared going ashore while she was there. Eventually, she made the climb back to the snowfield, scarred, missing plumage, with a limp gait. Her lineage is still tracked by outsiders.

#### The Dock

The dock is now quite capable, able to dock, unload, and load three ships in a day. It also has a shipbreaker yard to dismantle ships and a whaler's yard where whalers process their catch during the year they are stranded there.

#### The Steps

The Steps were built into the new cliffs. Besides having stairs and elevators to go up and down, the Steps also provide housing for the outsider population of Blue Hold. The snow and rock ice is reinforced and supplemented with outsider stone and metals. An important consideration is using stone and wood to insulate native materials from heat—a wood-backed stone slab, where wood interfaces with the snow and ice while stone absorbs most of the heat. Structurally, the Steps are two 10-story-tall towers.

#### The Warehouse

The Warehouse sits on top of the towers, which act as support pillars. Otherwise, the weight of the ore being stored would compact the snow into ice and cause instabilities. The warehouse has four massive cranes that lower ore down to the dock. It also has a furnace for smelting and ore processing, heavily insulated to prevent melting through the floor. Housing in the warehouse is the best in Blue Hold—for officers, managers, and supervisors. Unlike housing in the Steps, these quarters don't have to worry about melting the walls, so they have proper fireplaces, hearths, and kitchens.

### The Frost Rush Trail

The Frost Rush Trail is relatively simple with minimal infrastructure. There are waystations along it, maintained by both outsiders and Imoh. However, the trail has environmental impact: the heavy, ore-laden sleds apply pressure on the snowpack. Eventually, somewhere deep in the snowpack, an ice sheet forms. Any arable snow in the area can become barren, as the ice sheet prevents roots from reaching the soil.

## Weapons: Chucks

### Imoh-Produced Chucks

Imoh-produced chucks are not used in combat but rather for performance. Still, they can technically be used in combat. Because of their isolation and peaceful environment, Imoh-produced chucks have never been used in combat. They are built with soft woods and rope.

### Outsider-Produced Chucks

Chucks are produced in the rest of Threa, but not many. Since the isolated Imoh are the ones that receive an affinity boost from using chucks, no one outside can take full advantage of them. Armories in Threa find little reason to make chucks besides as novelty weapons. Unlike Imoh-produced chucks, armory-produced chucks are capable weapons, built with chains and hard woods or metals. If only they could be wielded by an Imoh to be used to their full potential.
