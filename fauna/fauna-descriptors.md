# Fauna Descriptors

A reference for describing Threan fauna. Each animal can be tagged with one descriptor per category. Some categories allow multiple tags where applicable (e.g., a bear is both a Walker and a Swimmer).

---

## 1) Presence

How the animal responds to threats and competition. Its willingness to hold ground.

- **Apex:** Stands its ground. Hard to intimidate or displace. Applies to predators and prey alike. [Lion, Elephant]
- **Stout:** Can chase off weaker threats and contest space or food, but yields when something clearly stronger shows up. [Wolverine, Honey badger, Goat]
- **Skittish:** Hair-trigger exit behavior. If something feels wrong, it disengages fast — flee, curl, smoke, drop-tail, whatever it has. [Rabbit, Deer, Lemming]

---

## 2) Size

How large the animal is relative to a person.

- **Critter:** Hand-sized and smaller, up to house-kitten scale. [Rat, Sparrow, Insects]
- **Creature:** Cat-to-anteater scale. [Goat, Coyote, Snow leopard]
- **Beast:** Large, but doesn't tower over a person. [Deer, Bison, Bear]
- **Theros:** Towers over a person. Megafauna presence. [Giraffe, Elephant, Whale, Brontosaur]

---

## 3) Environment

Where the animal lives long-term. Not where it can visit, but where it must return to.

- **Terrestrial:** Lives on land. Can enter water, but must return to land long-term. [Dog, Cat, Moose]
- **Marine:** Lives in water. Can go on land, but must return to water long-term. [Fish, Seal, Whale, Sea turtle]
- **Aquatic:** Can live long-term on both land and water. Commonly uses both. [Frog, Salamander, Crocodile, Hippo]

---

## 4) Locomotion

How the animal moves. An animal can have multiple locomotion tags. These attach to the environment tag — a Terrestrial animal that can also swim gets Walker + Swimmer. A Marine animal that can go on land gets Swimmer + Walker.

- **Walker:** Can move effectively on land. [Horse, Goat, Snake, Seal]
- **Swimmer:** Can move effectively in water. [Fish, Penguin, Bear]
- **Flyer:** Can move through air. [Bat, Duck, Flying fish]

---

## 5) Diet

What the animal eats.

- **Carnivore:** Eats other animals. [Lion, Crocodile, Orca]
- **Herbivore:** Eats plants. [Goat, Deer, Rabbit]
- **Omnivore:** Eats both animals and plants. [Raccoon, Bear, Pig]
- **Lumenvore:** Gains nutrition from sunlight. Photosynthesis or solar feeding. [Chikorita (Pokémon), Sun-sail lizard (fantasy)]
- **Lithivore:** Can ingest inorganic material — rocks, dirt, clay — as meaningful food or supplement. [Gorons (Zelda), Goat (clay/salt)]

---

## 6) Feeding

How the animal gets its food. Separate from what it eats.

- **Hunter:** Seeks out live prey. [Lion, Wolf, Crocodile]
- **Scavenger:** Seeks out dead animals or leftovers. [Vulture, Hyena]
- **Grazer:** Feeds across vast edible surfaces or dense food fields. The carnivore equivalent is filter feeding. [Cow, Bison, Baleen whale]
- **Browser:** Selective harvesting from patches — leaves, shrubs, shoots. The herbivore equivalent of hunting. [Goat, Giraffe]
- **Forager:** Searches for scattered edible tidbits. [Raccoon, Boar]
- **Parasite:** Feeds from a living host over time. [Tick, Lamprey]
- **Basker:** Stays still to gain nutrition. Usually tied to lumenvore solar charging. [Sea sponge, Chikorita (Pokémon)]

---

## 7) Activity

When the animal is awake.

- **Diurnal:** Awake during the day. [Eagle, Zebra]
- **Nocturnal:** Awake at night. [Bat, Owl]
- **Cathemeral:** Active and resting throughout both day and night. No fixed schedule. [Lion, Domestic cat]

---

## 8) Human Relationship

How the animal relates to people. A people-centered tag — defined by impact, not by the animal's intent.

- **Pest:** Causes problems for people. [Raccoon, Rat, Locust]
- **Boon:** Generally helpful or beneficial to people — pollination, cleanup, pest control, protection, etc. [Bee, Ladybug, Vulture]

---

## 9) Origin

Whether the animal belongs in the region it is found in.

- **Native:** From that place. [Local deer, Local songbird]
- **Invasive:** Brought into that region and spreads or causes disruption. [Island rats, Rabbits in Australia]

---

## 10) Grouping

How the animal organizes socially. The scale runs from individual to undifferentiated mass.

- **Solitary:** Lives alone. Either one parent raises the young, or the young are on their own from the start. [Polar bear, Tiger]
- **Partner:** Mated pair together. May raise young together. [Swans, Eagles]
- **Pack:** Small coordinated group. Members need not be related. Simple social structure — an alpha and hierarchy stemming from that. [Wolf, Wild dog]
- **Tribe:** Small coordinated group with complex roles — sentry, foragers, soldiers, etc. Members are related. [Monkeys, Meerkats]
- **Herd:** Large multi-family group. The individual still matters. Group defense is a thing. [Zebra, Bison]
- **Flock:** Large group where the group matters more than the individual. Stragglers are abandoned. A predator catches one and the flock scatters rather than assists. [Migratory geese, Starlings, Fish schools]
- **Swarm:** Undifferentiated mass. The individual doesn't matter. Loss doesn't change group behavior. A predator catches one and the swarm doesn't stop. [Locusts, Krill]

---

## 11) Reproduction

How the young enter the world.

- **Born:** Live birth. [Kangaroo, Horse]
- **Hatch:** Egg hatch. [Platypus, Sea turtle]

---

## 12) Year-scale Pattern

How the animal's behavior shifts across the year.

- **Yearround:** Active year-round. No major seasonal phase-shift. [Dog, Deer]
- **Seasonal:** Strong changes by season — coat, migration, mating behavior, etc. [Arctic fox, Emperor penguin]
- **Hibernating:** Sleeps through a season. [Bear, Groundhog]
- **Emergent:** Long inactivity with sudden periodic surges. [Cicadas]

---

## 13) Outer Covering

What covers the animal's skin. An animal can have multiple coverings on different parts of its body.

- **Furred:** Covered in fur. [Dog, Cat]
- **Feathered:** Covered in feathers. [Eagle, Duck]
- **Scaled:** Covered in scales. [Snake, Salmon]
- **Plated:** Solid armor-like surface. [Turtle, Armadillo, Pangolin]
- **Slick:** Mucus or goo coated. [Frog, Salamander]
- **Bald:** Exposed skin. [Human, Sphynx cat, Vulture head]

---

## 14) Range

How the animal relates to space and territory.

- **Territorial:** Has a defined, defended area. [Lion, Goose (nest season)]
- **Roaming:** Moves about without a defended territory. [Goat, Elephant]
- **Nesting:** Has a fixed place — nest, burrow, hive, den — but doesn't defend a wider territory. [Bees, Prairie dogs, Most birds]
- **Migrant:** Moves to far-away places. Regular long-distance movement. [Migratory birds, Whales]

---

## 15) Temperament

How the animal behaves toward people. Defined by the balance between approach and fight/flee response. Any temperament can flip into fighting if the animal is cornered and cannot escape.

- **Monster:** Will approach and fight people. Actively hunts or targets people. [Godzilla, Jason, Kraken, Curse-wolf ghost]
    
- **Hostile:** Won't approach. If provoked or approached, more likely to fight than flee. Not about power — a goose will fight if you're two inches closer. Context-dependent — a bear after feasting on salmon is placid, a bear near its pups is hostile. [Goose, Hippo, Bear]
    
- **Bold:** Will approach and intrude. More likely to fight than flee, but will still flee. A monkey walks up, grabs your drink, and runs. [Raccoon, Monkey]
    
- **Placid:** Won't approach, but can be approached. Won't move out of the way. More likely to fight than flee if annoyed. The manatee lazes on the beach while you walk by; once you're annoying, it chases you off. [Manatee, Baleen whale]
    
- **Docile:** Will approach and can be approached. Won't fight or flee. High tolerance — can be pushed, handled, even roughed up without triggering aggression. Unfortunately, this can lead to mistreatment. [Horse, Dog]
    
- **Wary:** May approach cautiously, but flees when spotted or pressed. The coyote tiptoes toward your sandwich, but when you see it, mission abort. [Coyote, Deer]
    
- **Flee:** Will not approach and won't be approached. Flees rather than fights. [Rabbit]
    
- **Cornered:** Universal override. If escape is impossible, almost any temperament can flip into fighting.
    

---

## 16) Visibility

How detectable the animal is. Defined by the relationship between build (passive) and behavior (active).

- **Brazen:** Doesn't hide. Moves openly with no concern for who's watching. [Bear, Hyena, Manatee]
- **Elusive:** Hard to detect by default due to build — quiet movement, camouflage, low visual signature. Not behavioral, just how they're made. A cat walks more silently than a dog. An owl flies more quietly than other birds. A single zebra is hard to see, even if the herd is obvious. [Tiger, Owl, Leopard, Zebra]
- **Covert:** Actively maintains concealment while moving. Behavior-driven hiding and mimicry. Goes out of its way to avoid detection — a stick bug carries sticks, pretends to sway, performs. [Stick bug, Octopus, Chameleon, Meerkat (when hiding)]
- **Blaring:** Makes its presence known through conspicuous display, sound, or color. "Notice me." [Wasp, Skunk, Coral snake, Guard dog (barking), Peacock]

---

## Domestic Roles

Separate from the wild descriptors. These describe the function of animals kept by people. An animal may serve multiple domestic roles.

### Companions

- **Loyal Companion:** Trainable, working-capable, highly attached. Guard, hunting, scout, and other active roles. [Dogs]
- **Companion:** Pest control, stealth, independent. [Cats]
- **Decorative Pet:** Kept for ambient presence, ornament, or status display. [Songbirds, Mouse, Snakes, Spiders]

### Working

- **Draft:** Pulls loads. [Oxen, Horses]
- **Pack:** Carries loads. [Llama, Horses]
- **Mount:** Can be ridden. [Horses]
- **Guard:** Used to guard, protect, attack, and alert. [Dog, Goose]
- **Scout:** Used to look ahead. [Dog, Falcon]
- **Hunter:** Assists in hunting. [Dog, Falcon, Ferret]
- **Sleuth:** Assists in searching. [Dog, Pig]

### Farming

Products harvested from the animal. Divided by whether the animal survives the harvest (renewable) or not (terminal).

**Renewable — animal lives, produces again:**

- **Wool:** Produces wool or other fibers for textile. [Sheep, Silkworm]
- **Feathers:** Produces feathers specifically. [Peacock, Bird of paradise, Eagle]
- **Milk:** Produces milk. [Cow]
- **Egg:** Produces eggs. [Chicken]
- **Honey:** Produces honey. [Bee]
- **Horns:** Produces horns, antlers, or other hard components while alive. Regrows or is shed. [Deer, Elk]

**Terminal — animal is killed for the product:**

- **Meat:** Muscle and fat bulked up to be eaten. While any animal can be eaten, this role is for those kept specifically to be eaten. [Cow, Chicken, Turkey, Salmon, Pig]
- **Pelt:** Used to produce furs and leather. One-time harvest. [Cow, Rabbit]
- **Bones:** Hard components harvested after death. Unlike horns, this is the last the animal will produce. [Cattle, Fish]
